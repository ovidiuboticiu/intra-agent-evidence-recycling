# Scientific Integrity Principle

**Adopted:** 2026-09-28

IAER follows a fail-open scientific transparency rule:

> We take responsibility for both successful results and mistakes. We do not preserve favorable claims merely because they were previously published, and we do not hide, delete, or silently rewrite errors, failed hypotheses, invalid runs, methodological weaknesses, or later corrections.

Operationally, this means:

- historical artifacts remain preserved unless removal is required for legal, privacy, or security reasons;
- corrections are explicit, dated, and versioned;
- a claim that can no longer be responsibly supported is withdrawn or narrowed in public, with the reason stated;
- numerical or empirical results that survive audit are preserved even when surrounding interpretation or novelty claims change;
- failed, aborted, invalid, or ineligible experiments remain part of the audit trail;
- later evidence may weaken, overturn, or strengthen earlier conclusions, and the public record should show that evolution;
- novelty, mechanism, generality, and replication claims are treated separately from raw empirical results;
- uncertainty is reported rather than converted into a stronger claim for presentation value.

For IAER specifically, this principle means that any correction to the historical novelty/priority wording will be made transparently, without altering the original frozen data or silently rewriting the historical publication record.

Scientific responsibility remains with the human author. AI-assisted analysis, drafting, coding, and auditing do not transfer that responsibility.
